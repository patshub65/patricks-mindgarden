## The brief

Frachtwerk is not a typical IT consulting company. They operate on New-Work principles — flat hierarchies, shared ownership, radical transparency. The problem: their old website looked like every other IT company. Logo wall, lead-gen CTAs, a carousel of enterprise case studies.

They didn't need leads. They needed people who actually wanted to work this way.

As senior web designer at Jut-so, I led the visual design on the redesign — working closely with a UX lead and developers to rebuild from the values up.

## The challenge

Translating company culture into web design is harder than it sounds. "Transparency" as a principle is abstract. "New-Work" is a term that can mean anything. The brief was essentially: make the website feel like the company — without resorting to stock photography of people laughing in open-plan offices.

Alongside that: breaking away from IT company conventions without losing clarity. Tech audiences have expectations. Violating all of them at once doesn't build trust.

## Process

**Discovery and research** — We began by analyzing Frachtwerk's existing brand assets and company values, interviewing stakeholders, and looking at how other progressive tech companies communicated their culture. We studied what "New-Work" actually meant in practice for them: different from the co-working aesthetic, grounded in real operational decisions.

**Concept development** — Working with the UX lead to refine wireframes and content structure, we developed a visual strategy built around two ideas: gradient as a stand-in for transparency (light shifting through surfaces), and texture as a humanising counterweight to tech precision. The mood boards ran from tactile printed matter to architectural photography — anything that felt considered, not corporate.

**Visual design** — We evolved Frachtwerk's existing brand colors into modern gradient styles. Grain and texture appeared throughout — enough to read as handmade without sacrificing legibility. Geometric shapes were adapted and blended with photography in ways that broke the standard hero/body/footer template.

**Implementation** — Worked directly with developers to ensure the designs held under real content and real devices. Frachtwerk needed to be able to update hero sections themselves, so the template system was designed for flexibility from day one.

## Key design decisions

**No logo wall.** The typical IT company home page announces credibility through clients. We removed it entirely. Frachtwerk's homepage leads with culture: blog posts, employer reviews, the texture of what it's actually like to work there.

**Dark and light mode.** Not for user preference — as a design statement. The toggle between dark and light surfaces made transparency literal. Blend modes let photography and UI coexist without either dominating.

**Navigation that reflects priorities.** The nav structure was rebuilt around what Frachtwerk wanted to be known for, not the standard product/services/about hierarchy.

**Custom UI elements.** Designed specifically for this brand, not adapted from a component library. The result reads as intentional rather than assembled.

## Outcome

Frachtwerk's team said the new site was the first time their digital presence matched how they described themselves internally. Engagement with the "get involved" section — their talent pipeline — measurably improved.

The site stood out from its category. Which, for a company whose competitive advantage is being unlike other IT firms, was exactly the point.
