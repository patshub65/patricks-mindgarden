Chikai was a joint venture between two Web3 companies, targeting developers building decentralized products. The brief called for something beyond a typical startup brand — they needed an identity that could double as a recruitment signal, attracting developers who genuinely believed in the cause.

The work started with research. I ran UX interviews with lead developers to get under the surface of what the brand needed to communicate — not just aesthetically, but in terms of values, personality, and the kind of people it needed to reach. What surfaced was a clear positioning opportunity: not another Web3 project promising a future, but a team of committed rebels actively fighting centralization.

That framing became the creative backbone. The narrative drew from the rebel alliance archetype — scrappy, principled, building something the establishment didn't want built. It resonated immediately with the developers in the research sessions.

![Chikai brand identity and visual system](/images/creating/chikai/detail-01.webp)

The visual direction followed through on that positioning: 8-bit pixel game aesthetics gave the brand a distinct, subcultural texture that read as both technical and countercultural. It signalled craft and irreverence in equal measure — exactly the right register for an audience who would spot anything generic from a distance.

![Chikai website and brand application](/images/creating/chikai/detail-02.png)

Deliverables included the full brand identity system, a messaging framework built around the rebel positioning, and a website that brought both together. The result successfully communicated the venture's mission while meeting its developer recruitment objectives.
