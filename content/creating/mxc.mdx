## The brief

MXC Foundation builds the infrastructure behind MatchX — blockchain-based IoT products connecting physical devices to decentralised networks. Their roadmap included expansion into the Metaverse. Their website, at the time, looked like neither.

Working with the team at Loominate, we took on the full rebrand: corporate identity from logo to design system, and a ground-up redesign of mxc.org.

## The challenge

MXC operates in a category that makes visual communication genuinely difficult. Blockchain and IoT are abstract concepts — you can't photograph them. The metaverse ambitions added another layer of complexity: they wanted to feel future-facing, but not in a way that erased the trust built by their existing product ecosystem.

Key tensions:
- Reimagining the brand while maintaining roots recognisable to existing users
- Translating complex technology into a visual language that felt accessible
- Designing an extensive, multi-page website with diverse content types
- Building a cohesive design system clean enough for Webflow handover

## Process

**Brand Discovery workshop** — We began with a structured session exploring MXC's 20-year vision, core principles, and competitive positioning. Two things emerged clearly: their roadmap toward the metaverse was central, not peripheral; and their most engaged audience had strong affinities with gaming aesthetics — CyberPunk, Vaporwave, the particular blend of retrofuturism you get from late-night electronics markets in Tokyo.

Brand promise we aligned behind: *"To bridge the gap between the real and virtual world, by connecting devices and people via social networks."*

![MXC brand direction and visual system](/images/creating/mxc/detail-01.png)

**Defining the visual style** — We built several StyleScapes exploring Vaporwave, CyberPunk, and more abstracted futurist directions. For the logo redesign, we worked with minimalist design principles, sci-fi geometric forms (the hexagon, which is structurally relevant to MXC's network topology), and gradient palettes that felt like bioluminescence or late-night neon.

**Comprehensive branding** — An in-house logo sketch from the MXC team became the unexpected foundation for the final mark. We developed it into a complete style guide: brand elements evoking a retrofuturist feel, a carefully weighted color palette spanning navy to turquoise on dark backgrounds and bright accents on light, and guidelines that would survive handover to a new internal team.

Color palette: `#0049FF` · `#6300FF` · `#F43178` · `#32D8A1`

**Website redesign** — Divided across four phases: content development (stakeholder interviews, content adaptation), low-fidelity wireframing (iterated with client), high-fidelity visual design in Figma (immersive, retro-futuristic elements throughout), and development handover with a staging environment for testing.

![MXC website and brand application](/images/creating/mxc/detail-02.png)

## The result

The before/after is stark. The old site could have belonged to any IoT firm from 2018. The new one reads like an invitation to something. Retro-futuristic without being costume — grounded in the actual logic of what MXC builds, expressed in a visual language with genuine ambition.

One of the harder rebrands I've worked on, and one of the more satisfying ones to ship.
