## The problem

Five tools, ten threads, and no one wants to be first to ask. By the time a plan forms, the moment's gone — even though 1 in 4 adults say they have no close friends to call. Spontaneous plans die in the gap between *I'm free* and *who's in?*, because making the first move is socially expensive and logistically annoying.

Sponti makes "I'm free, who's in?" a ten-second gesture. You light a flare, your circles see it, people join. That's the whole product.

## The role

I was Product Owner, UX/UI designer, and a share of the frontend. I led a four-person team — design, frontend, backend, and PM/QA — from a blank Miro board to a shipped React PWA in **16 days**.

The point of owning both product definition and UX wasn't tidiness. It meant the thing we scoped on Monday and the thing we designed on Wednesday were the same thing, with no translation loss in between.

**Stack:** React · Next.js · TypeScript · PWA. **Status:** in user testing, 228+ commits and counting.

## How I led the product

I ran the project as a tight three-step arc — discover, define, map — so we never designed ahead of what we'd actually agreed to build.

**Discover.** I facilitated the scoping workshops that defined the core MVP and translated the output into a PRD. Decisions got made in the room, not in a backlog later.

**Define.** From the MVP requirements I derived user stories, mapped the adjacent user tasks around each one, and prioritised the set for v1. Anything that wasn't load-bearing for the core loop got cut.

**Map.** I laid the screens out as a sitemap tree and built user flows directly from the stories — so the structure of the app was a consequence of the journeys, not a guess.

## The design system

The goal was to explore fast and ship consistent. I built Sponti's interface on **shadcn/ui** — accessible, unstyled primitives restyled to the brand. One source of truth for tokens kept every surface consistent while we moved quickly, and accessibility came for free rather than as a retrofit.

AI did the exploring before we committed to any one direction:

- **Variant** — scroll a feed of brand and layout directions and react to them, fast.
- **Claude Design** — wireframe the key screens and generate variations.
- **Figma Make** — turn a chosen direction into editable mockups.

## Design to code

AI did the bulk of the work. We did the steering — and that distinction is the whole story. This wasn't vibe coding. We used **Claude Code** and **Codex** to pressure-test the architecture and prototype the tricky components before building the real thing, then reviewed, corrected, and integrated everything by hand.

**What we built:**

- Map and calendar in React — one data source, two views.
- The "light a flare" creation flow, end to end.
- The join-a-flare flow.
- Send and accept connection requests.
- Friend circles, including custom circles.
- Privacy UI with re-share controls.
- Notification and account preferences.

**How we steered the AI:**

- Claude Code and Codex to define the architecture and spike components before implementing them for real.
- AI-assisted tests and audits — accessibility and code review — before anything merged.

## Outcome

Sponti shipped as a working React PWA in 16 days and is currently in user testing, with 228+ commits and ongoing development. It's live at [sponti.fun](https://sponti.fun), and the code is on GitHub.

The lesson stuck: with AI doing the bulk and a human doing the steering, a four-person team can take a product from blank board to shipped in the time it used to take to write the spec — *if* someone owns the definition tightly enough that the speed doesn't turn into drift.
