Creative studios occupy an interesting position in the identity landscape. They're single entities that need to read as authoritative, but they also need to feel open enough that clients can imagine their own work flourishing inside the brand.

Keyko's identity was built around that duality. The wordmark is confident and distinctive — made to hold up on a business card, a pitch deck, and a studio website without apology. But the system around it has flexibility built in: colour adaptations, layout variations, a template language that creative collaborators could actually use.

The work covered logo, visual identity system, stationery, and brand guidelines. A concise set of outputs, done with care — which is usually the right answer for a creative practice that doesn't want its own branding to overshadow the work it produces.
