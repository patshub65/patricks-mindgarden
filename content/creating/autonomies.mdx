## The brief

Autonomies had a clear mission: empower electronic music artists and collectors through a decentralised NFT marketplace. The technology was there. What wasn't was a face — a brand and product experience that would make this feel like something worth caring about, not another Web3 pitch deck in UI form.

I came on as lead designer, partnering with Loominate. My scope covered the full design surface: brand identity, marketing materials, announcement landing page, and the web app UI itself.

## The challenge

Web3 products tend to solve for the technology before they solve for the person. NFT interfaces love to lead with wallets, gas fees, and blockchain jargon — and then wonder why only crypto-natives convert.

Our audience was different: electronic music collectors. People who know the feeling of flipping through a crate, finding something improbable, holding it. The challenge was designing an experience that spoke to *that* sensibility, not the DeFi crowd.

Alongside that: designing an intuitive browsing and purchasing flow for something that had no existing mental model. A digital record that isn't quite a streaming track, isn't quite a physical object, and is technically owned via a smart contract.

## Discovery

We started by immersing ourselves in the existing landscape — analyzing music platforms, NFT marketplaces, and record shop experiences side by side. The gap was obvious: everything in the NFT music space felt like a dashboard. Nothing felt like a shop.

User interviews with electronic music collectors and artists confirmed it. They wanted discovery. They wanted browsing to feel like browsing — unhurried, tactile, surprising.

## Brand direction

The visual language needed to feel like a record sleeve from a mid-90s label you've almost heard of. Not pastiche, but grounded in that aesthetic legacy: subdued colour palette pulled from faded vinyl sleeves, typography that referenced classic electronic music flyers, iconography that merged turntable shapes with more abstract digital forms.

The brand didn't try to look like a startup. It looked like it belonged in a shop.

## Product design

The homepage was designed as a digital record wall — the immediate visual metaphor for how physical record shopping works. You see the covers. You browse by feel. You click when something pulls you in.

Key interface decisions:

- **The Wall** — a grid of records, browsable by new releases, second-hand, or artist A–Z. The browsing logic before the purchase logic.
- **Ownership metadata** — surfaced clearly but not loudly. You could see who made it, when, and what you'd own. We stripped everything else.
- **Profile and social layer** — follow artists, build a collection, signal taste. The social graph as record shop regulars.

We ran several iterations focused on reducing friction without reducing information. In Web3 UX, the temptation is to explain everything. We did the opposite: explain only what earns trust at the moment you need it.

## Outcome

The project didn't make it to market — the broader NFT market contraction in 2023 paused Autonomies' roadmap. But the design work stands as one of the cleaner attempts I've seen at making Web3 feel like something a music lover would actually use. The brand had texture. The interface had rhythm.

Sometimes the brief is more interesting than the launch.
