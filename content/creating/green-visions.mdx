Green Visions Potsdam is a sustainability film festival with an unusual ambition: it combines film screenings with scientific knowledge sharing and a local artisanal market. Three distinct audience groups, three distinct content types, one website to serve all of them without anyone getting lost.

As part of the design team at Jut-so, I was heavily involved in the website design from brief to delivery.

## The challenge

The hardest constraint wasn't aesthetic — it was structural. How do you build a site that simultaneously serves the film enthusiast who wants to know which movies are showing, the researcher who wants to explore the science programme, and the local visitor who came for the market?

Most festival websites pick a primary user and build down from there. We decided to make the information architecture do the work instead.

![Green Visions festival site and category system](/images/creating/green-visions/detail-01.png)

## Process

We began by mapping user journeys for each audience segment separately — then looked for the patterns that let them coexist in the same navigation and content structure. Colour-coding became the central organising system: each content category (films, scientific knowledge, the market) had a distinct visual channel. A tagging system extended this into individual pages so users always knew which territory they were in.

The brand language came from the festival's logo. We extracted its core visual elements — the organic forms, the earthy palette — and extended them into a light-themed design with gradient overlays that felt like filtered daylight. Deliberately not corporate, deliberately warm.

Custom iconography was designed for each section: recognisable enough to work at small sizes in navigation, expressive enough to feel crafted at display scale.

![Green Visions navigation and iconography](/images/creating/green-visions/detail-02.jpg)

## Design features

**Colour-coded categories** — Films, Science, and Market each carried a distinct hue through the site. The system was simple enough to be immediately understood, consistent enough to build genuine wayfinding.

**Flexible content modules** — The same layout system needed to work for a film listing with a two-line title and screening times, and a scientific talk with an abstract and a linked paper. Modular components made this manageable.

**Practical UX for decision-making** — Ticket purchasing and schedule access were surfaced prominently for users who already knew what they wanted. Deeper exploration was available but didn't block the direct path.

## Outcome

A visually cohesive site that served its genuinely diverse audience without compromise. Multiple user groups navigated successfully to their respective content without feeling like they'd landed on the wrong page. The custom iconography and colour system gave the festival a design identity that felt like an extension of the event itself — conscious, local, unhurried.
